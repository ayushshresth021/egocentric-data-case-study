Proposed data product

Recovery & Repair Pack

Failure is only useful training data when the mistake, response, and successful recovery are separable.

What it is

Controlled failures inside familiar tasks

People perform the six EgoVerse flagship tasks while one or two small failures are introduced mid-take. The existing task definitions, scoring, and robot demonstrations make the pack immediately compatible with an established test harness.

Why it exists

The recovery boundary is the product

Whole demonstrations blur the action that caused a failure with the action that repaired it. Span labels let training pipelines mask, re-weight, or evaluate each phase independently—preserving rare recoveries without teaching the preceding mistake.

Why more successful demonstrations can still hurt performance ↗
6Flagship tasks
9,000Target episodes
1–2Failures per episode
External failure

Tipped container, dropped tool, occluded target, or interruption. The world changed; the operator’s preceding behavior remains valid.

Self-caused failure

Missed grasp or wrong-object selection. The causal action is labeled separately so its loss can be masked before the repair sequence.

Technical specification

DimensionTarget specificationAcceptance rule
Dataset unitDataset → session → task → episode → trajectory → event → timestep.Every timestep resolves to one versioned episode and task definition.
Episode scopeApproximately five minutes; 5–10 demonstrations per task setup; static and mobile work flagged.Clear start state, instruction, terminal state, and outcome.
ModalitiesStereo fisheye RGB, stereo-derived depth, and time-aligned IMU.No modality may silently drop inside a labeled recovery span.
Capture rigHead-mounted stereo fisheye; fixed 6 cm baseline; 1920 × 1200 at 30 FPS.Rig and firmware identity recorded with every session.
SynchronizationShared timestamps across video, depth, and IMU.Stream offset above 5 ms quarantines the session.
CalibrationIntrinsic and extrinsic calibration every 20 session-hours.Drift above 1.5 px removes the rig from capture.
Task variation40 × 60 cm workspace, randomized positions, up to 30 objects.Object layout and initial state recorded before each take.
Failure protocolOne or two planned failures with randomized type and timing; operators remain blind to the affected take.Failure source tagged as external or self-caused.
Recovery spansCause → onset → recognition → repair start → repair end → terminal outcome.Each boundary carries timestamp, confidence, and reviewer identity.
Timestep schemaObservation, actor state, action, environmental feedback, outcome, timestamp, calibration, and confidence.All required fields are queryable without replaying raw video.
Training controlsPer-span flags for copy, mask, down-weight, contrast, or evaluation-only use.Self-caused pre-failure actions default to masked.
VisibilityBoth hands visible in at least 95% of frames.Below 90% rejects the episode; intermediate cases enter review.
TrackingContinuous head pose and camera tracking through failure and repair.Lost tracking rejects the episode.
Curation“Busy segments only” filtering disabled across labeled failure and repair spans.No automated trim may remove causal context.
Adjudication20% double-labeled; third reviewer resolves disagreement.Boundary agreement κ ≥ 0.75 or the batch is relabeled.
ProvenanceOperator consent, site, device, task version, failure script, transforms, and label history.Every derived asset traces back to rights-cleared source media.
Dataset splitsHold out operators, sites, object instances, and failure variants.No identity or scene leakage across train, validation, and test.
